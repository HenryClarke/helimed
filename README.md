## Helimeds 🚁🚨

Air Ambulances in the UK are almost entirely (except 2 helicopters in Scotland) run as charities with no government funding. Each uses a unique 'Helimed' callsign, followed by 2 numbers. 
The information as to which organisations operates, what registrations, what callsigns and from where is public knowledge and available from a variety of sources, but is disparate. I wanted to see an overview of all Air Ambulances in the UK, but couldn't find one, so I've collated all the information here.

The data below is collated from a number of sources, including Wikipedia, the websites and social media of each organisation, along with various press releases and news stories.

The data may contain mistakes, but I believe represents an accurate picture of Air Ambulances in the UK. If you notice any mistakes, required updates, omissions, etc., please contact me at helimeds@henryclarke.uk to inform me, and I will update the table accordingly.

Below shows the organisation, helicopter home base, registration, callsign, aircraft type, and the operator of the helicopter.
- Some organisations 'operate' the helicopters themselves, they own the helicopters and provide their own pilots, but most organisations outsource this to a specialist aviation company, such as Babcock, Castle Air, Gama Aviation - all who provide the aircraft, crew, maintenance.
- Some helicopters may change on an ad-hoc basis during maintenance or other availability, and may receive a 'courtesy helicopter' with generic branding, but will use the callsign of the helicopter they are replacing. This means that whilst `HELIMED 24` will always belong to the Hampshire and Isle of Wight Air Ambulance, for example, it may not be always be the aircraft `G-HIOW`. 
- Aircraft types are 'generic' and are abstracted to the 'line' of helicopter, not the very specific model, it is meant as a simple comparison between organisations, to see which models are popular, etc.

All Air Ambulances have callsigns starting with 'Helimed' followed by a two-digit number, and optionally with the following suffix:
- 'Alpha', i.e. `Helimed 01 ALPHA`, when on time-critical tasking, such as on the way to an incident or to hospital with a patient
- 'Echo', a positioning flight, for example returning to base after an incident
- 'Zulu', training or other non-operational flight.

These suffixes communicate the urgency/priority of the flight, in addition to the fact it is an Air Ambulance from the 'Helimed' callsign.
